## Anti-Patterns Verdict — FAIL (reads as AI-generated)

LLM assessment: Judged as a brand surface, this is the modal AI-study-tool landing. Inverse test fits the category: "centered hero + pill badge + 3-step icon-card flow + two feature cards = restart." Tells: pill-badge hero eyebrow (restates the h1), rounded icon chips above every card heading (x5; "screams template"), identical-card grids (3 step cards + 2 mode cards), centered hero + two buttons + no real imagery. The restraint itself is correct (calm, Linear-disciplined brand) — the problem is restraint with generic scaffolding instead of a distinctive POV. Stripe/Vercel are restrained AND unmistakable; this isn't yet.

Deterministic scan: detect.mjs -> [] (exit 0). Reads code-level slop (none here); does NOT read brand distinctiveness or copy rules, so misses the template critique and the em-dashes. Clean scan, brand fail.

Visual overlays: No browser automation; no overlay. Fallback: source + scan only.

## Overall Impression

Competent, on-brand-colored, coherent with the app — but it describes the product in generic cards instead of showing the one thing that makes it special. The best things are the two mini-mockups (real flashcard, real "Why this card" callout) — buried at the bottom. Biggest opportunity: make the agent's visible reasoning the hero, and lead with it.

## What's Working

1. The mini-mockups — preview the actual product + the "Why this card" differentiator. Most distinctive, most honest part.
2. Clean, coherent restraint — reads as the same product as the app (same tokens, serif headings, warm palette).
3. Specific, human copy — "Good for 'I don't know where to start, just teach me.'"; accurate, buzzword-free mode descriptions.

## Priority Issues

[P1] Buries its differentiator; reads as a generic AI-SaaS template. Secondary audience = technical reviewers evaluating hand-built agentic engineering; a template signals "scaffolded fast." Per Design Principle 1, the agent is the story, but it's one card among several. Fix: lead with the agent reasoning — promote a live/animated "Why this card" callout into the hero. Distinctiveness within restraint. Command: bolder / shape.

[P2] Study and Tutor presented as co-equal, contradicting "Tutor leads." Everywhere in the app Tutor is the flagship/leading action; the landing's "Two ways to study" 2-up grid gives them equal weight. Fix: give Tutor primacy (larger/first/hero demo), Study secondary. Command: layout.

[P2] Icon-chip-above-heading on all 5 cards is a template tell ("screams template"). Fix: drop/rethink the chips; let type + mockups carry the cards. Command: distill.

[P3] Three em-dashes (&mdash; x3) violate the no-em-dash rule. Fix: commas/colons/periods/parentheses. Command: clarify.

[P3] Hero pill badge restates the h1 ("Adaptive AI study tutor"). Fix: cut or replace with voice. Command: typeset.

## Persona Red Flags

Jordan (First-Timer): Well served — how-it-works + two-mode explanation make it clear. Clarity is a strength.

Casey (Mobile): Responsive, 44px CTAs on touch. text-[10px] mockup labels are tiny but decorative. Minor.

## Minor Observations

- "Step 1/2/3" numbering is fine — a genuine ordered sequence (the earned use, not the banned reflex); chevrons reinforce it.
- text-[10px] labels are below the comfortable mobile floor; fine as chrome, not for real content.
- No real product screenshots — faux mockups stand in; real/animated UI would convince the reviewer audience more.

## Questions to Consider

- What if the hero WAS the product — a live, animated "Why this card" callout — instead of a headline describing it?
- If a visitor saw only the first fold, would they know the differentiator is an agent that reasons, or just "another flashcard app"?
- Does "Two ways to study" need a balanced 2-up, or a clear protagonist (tutor) and supporting act (study)?
